## Negative / unexpected results

**The temperature ablation itself is inconclusive, for an unexpected
and more important reason than temperature.** Raw paired result on the
21-problem band-1 subset: oracle-vs-best-fixed gap goes from 14.29pp
(temp=0.8) to 4.76pp (temp=1.0), paired difference -9.52pp, BCa 95% CI
`[-38.10pp, -4.76pp]` (excludes zero). **This number should NOT be read
as "G1/SELECT are temperature-sensitive."** Investigation (prompted by
the gap being ~4x larger on this 21-problem MATH-500-only subset than
Day 12's 3.97pp figure for the full 151-problem band-1, both
benchmarks) found the real cause:

- `controller/oracle_labels.py`'s `_majority_correct`/`_any_correct`
  silently drop failed extractions (`length_truncated` etc.) from the
  vote — reasonable when most samples succeed, but on truncation-heavy
  problems this means "the majority" is computed among however few
  samples happened to produce a boxed answer at all, not a real
  majority-of-32 vote. Hand-verified on `test/geometry/615.json`: 31/32
  samples truncated, the 1 survivor was correct, so
  `sample_correct=True` — technically correct per the code's own
  definition, not what "majority vote" means in the brief's intent.
- **P1-wide check by band** (`notes/scratch/day13_extraction_survivorship_check.py`,
  N=32 pools only, n=521 problems) confirms this is sharply
  band-concentrated, not a general problem:

  | band | mean successful extractions /32 | % problems with ≤3 survivors |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0 (hardest 20%) | 0.21 | 99% |
  | 1 (moderately-hard) | 7.06 | 42% |
  | 2 | 25.96 | 0% |
  | 3 | 31.81 | 0% |
  | 4 (easiest 40%) | 32.00 | 0% |

- **This means two of 2026-08-24's E2 landscape claims need a caveat,
  not a retraction:**
  1. Band 0's "0.000 ceiling for every action, including the oracle,
     nothing works there regardless of strategy" is likely
     mischaracterized — at 99% of band-0 problems producing on average
     0.21/32 boxed answers, this reads much more like "the 1024-token
     budget is too short to finish these problems" than "these
     problems are unsolvable by any strategy." Different implication
     for whether SEARCH or a longer budget could help there.
  2. Band 1's "~4pp of the gap concentrated here" is likely partly (maybe
     mostly) a survivorship artifact from the same mechanism, at a
     smaller scale (42% of problems affected vs. band 0's 99%) — not
     clean reasoning-headroom signal.
- **What is NOT called into question:** the aggregate G1-failed and
  SELECT-0.8%-win-rate verdicts. Both are dominated by bands 2-4 (60% of
  all problems), which are completely clean (0% affected, near-ceiling
  accuracy, real majorities over ~26-32 samples each). The two frozen
  decisions stand; the *band-landscape mechanism story* built on top of
  them needs a correction before it goes to the mentor.
- **Secondary finding salvaged from the same data — this IS a real,
  clean answer to the original ablation question, just not the one
  planned:** mean successful extractions on band 1 barely moved between
  temperatures (2.67/32 at 0.8 -> 2.81/32 at 1.0) — temperature is not
  the lever on the truncation problem. `max_tokens` is. If there's ever
  time to revisit band 0/1 behavior, that's the parameter to ablate,
  not temperature.
- **Did not rerun the ablation on a "clean" band (e.g. band 2) as a
  fallback.** Checked first: band 2's own gap is already only 0.66pp
  with 0% survivorship-affected problems (99.3% best-fixed accuracy
  already) — there is no band with both a meaningful gap and clean
  extraction; band 1 is the only band with real gap, and it's exactly
  the confounded one. A band-2 rerun would trivially show "no effect"
  because there's nothing there to detect, at the cost of another real
  ~50-60min generation run. Decided this is itself worth reporting
  honestly rather than working around by cherry-picking a band where
  nothing can be found either way.

## The max_tokens angle — the biggest finding of the day, prompted by
## the user's own suspicion that the controller looked "shit"
Mid-session, user raised: most problems resolve to STOP or ABSTAIN --
is the controller actually doing anything? Traced this to the same root
cause as the temperature-ablation finding above, then tested it
directly rather than just theorize: **does raising max_tokens actually
recover real signal on the problems currently scored as totally
unwinnable, or is band 0 genuinely a reasoning-difficulty ceiling?**

- Selected the 6 most extreme MATH-500 problems available: **pass@1=0.000
  AND 0/32 successful extractions** at the frozen max_tokens=1024 --
  completely dead, zero information, at the current budget.
  (`notes/scratch/day13_maxtokens_select_fast.py`; the first attempt,
  `day13_maxtokens_select.py`, computing bands on the full P1+P2 754
  problems, hung/was slow for >11min with no output -- likely the
  OlympiadBench gold-fetch or a slow equivalence check -- killed and
  replaced with a faster P1-only version since OlympiadBench data
  wasn't needed for this check anyway.)
- Regenerated all 6 at **max_tokens=4096** (temperature held at the
  frozen 0.8 -- deliberately NOT combined with the earlier temperature
  ablation, to isolate max_tokens as the only changed variable).
  `configs/pools/day13-maxtokens-ablation-4096.yaml`. 192/192 samples,
  0 failures, 43.5min.
- **Result: 6/6 flipped from ABSTAIN to a real action.** Mean
  successful-extraction rate went from 0% to ~75% (145/192 total across
  the 6). 5 of 6 resolved all the way to STOP (the CHEAPEST action --
  even a 4-sample probe majority becomes correct); the 6th resolved to
  SAMPLE (needs the full 32-sample vote, but still resolves). One
  problem (`counting_and_probability/10.json`) went from 0/32 to a
  perfect 32/32.
- **This is a real correction to the E2 landscape's headline claim, not
  just a caveat.** "The hardest 20% has a 0.000 ceiling for every
  action, including the oracle, nothing works there regardless of
  strategy" should read **"...at max_tokens=1024"** -- a fundamentally
  different, more optimistic claim. These are not unsolvable problems;
  the model was being cut off mid-derivation on every single sample,
  every single time, on this 6-problem sample.
- **Direct answer to the "controller is shit" concern:** a real chunk of
  the 36% ABSTAIN mass in the oracle action distribution is not "no
  headroom exists to allocate toward" -- it's "the harness never gave
  the model room to finish." The controller's actual decision-relevant
  workload at a properly-sized token budget is likely very different
  (more STOP/SAMPLE-actionable, less genuine ABSTAIN) than what's
  currently measured at 1024 tokens. This doesn't mean the controller
  implementation is bad -- Detective's per-class weakness on rare
  classes (SAMPLE/SELECT) is still real and separate -- but it does mean
  part of what looked like "nothing to do" was a budget artifact, not a
  ceiling.
- **Does NOT simply contradict Day 3's aggregate truncation finding**
  ("at 4x budget, OlympiadBench-A was still 50% truncated -- heavily
  diminishing returns"). Day 3 measured an aggregate average across all
  difficulty levels on OlympiadBench-A; this measured the single most
  extreme tail of MATH-500 specifically (pass@1=0.000, 0/32 extractions)
  -- a much more targeted, and much more informative, slice. Both can be
  true: diminishing returns in aggregate, near-total recovery on the
  specific dead tail.
- **n=6 is small and hand-picked (the worst case, deliberately) -- not
  a claim that ALL of band 0 recovers this cleanly, or that 4096 is the
  right value.** But 6/6 flipping, with 5/6 resolving all the way to the
  cheapest action, is strong enough signal on its own that this isn't
  noise. Worth a larger, systematic max_tokens sweep across all of band
  0 (and maybe band 1) before the report's headline numbers are final --
  flagging as a new, high-priority open item, not something to bury as
  a footnote next to the temperature-ablation caveat.

## Follow-up: does more max_tokens rescue SELECT specifically?
Direct test of a natural follow-up question after the band-0 max_tokens
result: does raising max_tokens preserve/strengthen a real SELECT
signal, or does it just resolve truncation-confounded cases to
STOP/SAMPLE like band 0 did? Tested on the 2 known, canonical P1
SELECT-oracle problems that were also truncation-confounded
(`number_theory/1055.json`, `precalculus/881.json`, both 2/32
extractions at 1024 tokens) -- regenerated at max_tokens=4096, temp held
at 0.8. `configs/pools/day13-select-maxtokens-ablation.yaml`. 64/64
samples, 0 failures, 7.2min.

**Result: both flipped to STOP with perfect 32/32 extraction** --
SELECT's apparent win disappeared entirely rather than strengthening.
Confirms the mechanism from the band-0 test even more cleanly: these
"SELECT wins" were truncation noise (2 lucky survivors), not real
disagreement-then-rescue cases; once the model can finish, the answer
is unanimous and majority-correct. **Answer: more tokens does not
rescue SELECT -- it makes SELECT's apparent wins vanish.** Combined with
the band-0 result (6/6 ABSTAIN -> mostly STOP, none to SELECT), this is
8/8 consistent evidence that max_tokens converts broken problems into
easy ones, never into genuinely SELECT-favorable ones. A fifth
independent confirmation that SELECT is genuinely rare in this data, not
a truncation or temperature artifact.

## Day 14 pulled forward: the real E5/G6 predictor evaluation (prompted
## by "is the controller useless, it always says STOP?")
Mid-session worry, pressed harder after the earlier controller
discussion: does Detective actually discriminate, or does it just
predict the majority class? The only number that existed
(93.6%/91.9%, `notes/scratch/day11_e0_thin_slice.py`) was an IN-SAMPLE
fit on the 100-problem dev slice, explicitly flagged in that script's
own comments as preliminary/not final -- real grouped-CV fitting is
Day 14's job per docs/brief.md section 16. Ran it for real, full scale,
under time pressure (3-4 real days left for the whole project):

- `notes/scratch/day13_e5_predictor_cv.py`: real grouped-by-problem,
  stratified-by-benchmark 5-fold CV (docs/brief.md section 16 Protocol),
  full P1+P2 scale (787 usable problems: 500 MATH-500 + 287/300
  OlympiadBench-A with valid gold + exactly N=32 -- more complete than
  Day 12's 254/300, likely because that script's gold-fetch loop could
  exit early; this one scans the full 674-row dataset unconditionally).
  Mirrors `DetectiveController`'s exact preprocessing (train-only NaN
  imputation, StandardScaler, multinomial LogisticRegression) via direct
  array CV rather than repeated single-shot fits.
- **Result: macro-AUROC = 0.8797 out-of-fold -- clears G6's accept bar
  (>=0.70) comfortably, not marginally. H2 accepts.** Per-class AUROC:
  STOP 0.9955, ABSTAIN 0.9598, SAMPLE 0.8642 (genuinely good -- real
  signal exists), SELECT 0.6994 (weak, n=9 support, structurally too
  rare for a robust number). Overall accuracy 90.1% vs. 54.6% for
  "always guess STOP" -- a real 35.4pp lift, not noise.
- **The honest, narrower version of "always predicts STOP":** SAMPLE's
  argmax recall is only 16.2% despite its AUROC being genuinely good
  (0.86) -- the model DOES carry real signal about SAMPLE, but the
  hard decision rule rarely lets it win against the dominant STOP/
  ABSTAIN priors. Not "no signal," a calibration/threshold issue.
- **Follow-up, same-day: `notes/scratch/day13_e5_predictor_cv_balanced.py`
  -- does `class_weight='balanced'` fix the SAMPLE recall issue?** Real
  trade-off, not a clean win: SAMPLE recall 16.2%->55.9% (up), but
  ABSTAIN recall 96.1%->65.7% (down), STOP recall 99.8%->90.5% (down),
  overall accuracy 90.1%->77.6% (down), macro-AUROC 0.880->0.852
  (roughly flat, as expected -- AUROC is threshold-independent).
  **SELECT recall stays 0% either way** -- confirms this is structural
  (9 examples total, ~1-2 per CV fold), not fixable by reweighting.
  **Decision: report the plain model as the headline G6/H2 result**
  (stronger overall, clears the bar cleanly); document the balanced
  variant as an honest ablation showing the SAMPLE/ABSTAIN trade-off
  exists, not a silent swap of the default.
- **Bottom line for the "is the controller useless" worry:** no. Under
  the real evaluation (not the optimistic in-sample number), Detective
  clears G6 comfortably with a real 35pp lift over the trivial floor.
  What's true and worth being honest about: it's much better at
  STOP/ABSTAIN than at the rare classes, SAMPLE has real-but-underused
  signal, and SELECT is unreachable at this sample size regardless of
  method -- consistent with, not contradicting, everything else found
  today (SELECT confirmed rare 5 independent ways now).
- **This is real Day-14 progress** (E5's core deliverable, H2 verdict),
  pulled forward under the 3-4-day time constraint rather than waiting.
  Still outstanding for a complete Day 14: H3 (needs the Fortune Teller
  pre-hoc embedding comparator, not yet run), the majority-class/
  fixed-agreement-threshold/difficulty-tier-oracle comparators from the
