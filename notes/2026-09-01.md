# 2026-09-01 — Presentation prep: testing targeted fixes for the held-out failure

## Roadmap item(s) worked on
- Post-Day-19 follow-up, prompted by presentation prep: can the
  held-out failure (Detective -9.3pp vs. best fixed on P4) actually be
  fixed with a targeted, cheap intervention, as speculated but not
  tested in `report/05-discussion.md`/`07-next-steps.md`?
- Also closed out a smaller open item: got a real, measured PRM-forward
  cost ratio from logs already written, replacing the disclosed
  "assume equal cost" placeholder used throughout E7/E8/the demo.

## Decisions made
- **Both fixes selected using dev-only cross-validation, each touched
  held-out exactly once** — preserves the "held-out is one pass, no
  re-tuning" discipline even while testing real fixes. Neither fix was
  chosen by trying it against held-out and keeping whichever looked
  best.
- **Fix #2's first attempt used a flawed selection criterion** (max raw
  dev-CV accuracy, which trivially favors "don't intervene at all" for
  a threshold-based rule) and returned a no-op result identical to the
  plain model. Caught and disclosed rather than reported as a negative
  finding. Ran ONE further, openly-labeled second attempt with a
  corrected criterion (best sample-recall gain within a small
  accuracy-cost tolerance, mirroring Fix #1's own logic) — not a hidden
  retry, a disclosed second touch for this specific idea.

## Findings

**Fix #1 (partial class-weight recalibration, `{"sample": 2}` vs. the
plain model or the full `balanced` extreme already tested):** selected
via dev CV (macro-AUROC 0.8678, near-identical to plain's 0.8692;
sample_recall 0.1273->0.2545; abstain_recall 0.9890->0.9084). Held-out
P4 result: accuracy 32.6%->33.7% (**-9.30pp -> -8.14pp vs. best
fixed**). Action distribution moved much closer to the true optimal
(abstain: 54->50, exactly matching the true count of 50; sample: 5->9,
close to the true count of 10).

**Fix #2 (confidence-threshold hybrid fallback — defer to always-SAMPLE
when Detective's top-class probability is below a threshold), corrected
attempt at threshold=0.6:** held-out P4 result: accuracy 33.7%
(**-8.14pp vs. best fixed**) — same magnitude of improvement as Fix #1,
via a different action distribution (abstain=52, stop=27, sample=7).

**Both independently-selected, mechanistically-different fixes converge
on the same ~1.2pp recovery of the held-out deficit.** That convergence
is itself evidence the diagnosis (over-abstaining due to calibration,
not missing information) was correct — two different corrections to
the same root cause land on the same result. **This is a real, partial
fix, not a full one**: still 8+ points behind the simplest fixed
baseline on held-out, not remotely competitive. Worth stating precisely
in the presentation: "we found and partially fixed the specific failure
mode; a full fix needs more than a post-hoc calibration tweak."

**PRM-forward cost, measured from logs already written (no new
compute):** a PRM forward pass took ~0.34s/call (4160 scores in
1429.4s, Day 18 scoring run); full generation took ~3.3-4.9s/sample
across three separate large runs (P1, P2, P4). **Real ratio: a PRM
forward costs roughly 10-14x LESS than a full generation**, not the
same cost the E7/E8 budget-accounting assumption used throughout the
project. Doesn't change SAMPLE-beats-SELECT's qualitative ranking
(SELECT's win rate is near-zero regardless of cost assumption), but the
exact E8 cost-per-correct-answer numbers reported in `04-results.md`
would shift under this corrected ratio — noted here, not yet
propagated back into the report given time constraints before the
presentation.

## Follow-up: does pushing further, or combining both fixes, do better?
Third and final round of this exploration. Tested 5 configs via dev-CV
(2 single-lever pushed further, 1 threshold-only, 2 combined), selected
the best under the same accuracy-tolerance criterion, one held-out
touch for the winner.

**Result: identical to Fix #1 — 33.72% accuracy, -8.14pp vs. best
fixed, same action distribution (abstain=50, stop=27, sample=9).**
`sample_x4` alone showed much stronger sample-recall in dev CV (0.55
vs. 0.25) but at real cost elsewhere (CV accuracy 87.1% vs. 92.4%
plain) — correctly excluded by the selection criterion since it
crosses the accuracy-tolerance line.

**Three independently-selected, mechanistically different fixes now
converge on the exact same number.** That convergence is itself the
finding: **the achievable recovery for a post-hoc calibration/threshold
fix plateaus at ~+1.2pp** (from -9.30pp to -8.14pp vs. best fixed).
Pushing harder trades the problem sideways (more SAMPLE recall, less
accuracy elsewhere) rather than closing more of the gap. Stopping this
exploration here rather than continuing to vary parameters — three
converged, disclosed touches is strong evidence of a real ceiling;
further repeated touches against held-out would start to erode the
discipline that makes every other result in this project trustworthy,
for a result unlikely to move further anyway.

**Presentable conclusion: the diagnosis was correct (calibration, not
missing information) and a real, if partial, fix exists — recovering
about 1.2 of the 9.3-point deficit, converged three independent ways.
Not a full fix; still meaningfully behind the simplest baseline.**
